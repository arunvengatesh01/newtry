ARTIFICIAL COLOURS

The dark side of artificial colours

  • Many synthetic colours are made from industrial chemical sources and add no nutritional value.
  • A 2007 University of Southampton study reported a link between mixtures of some colours and a preservative and increased hyperactivity in some children.
  • The EU requires a warning on foods containing six specified colours.
  • Some colours can trigger hives or asthma-like reactions in sensitive people.

Five colours commonly used in foods

Colour Possible reactions reported in some people
Tartrazine (E102, yellow) Hives or asthma-type reactions in sensitive people
Sunset Yellow FCF (E110) Skin or allergic reactions in sensitive people
Carmoisine (E122, red) Possible sensitivity reactions
Ponceau 4R (E124, red) Possible sensitivity reactions
Allura Red AC (E129) Possible sensitivity reactions

Newtry's intention: avoid artificial colours and let the colour come from ingredients and baking.
