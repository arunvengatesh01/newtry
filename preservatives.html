PRESERVATIVES

What are preservatives?

1. Definition

Preservatives are substances added to slow spoilage caused by microorganisms or oxidation. Examples include calcium propionate (E282), potassium sorbate (E202), sodium benzoate (E211), BHA (E320), BHT (E321) and TBHQ (E319).

2. Safety considerations

  • Some preservatives can cause sensitivity reactions in certain people.
  • Safety depends on the specific substance, permitted level, food type and exposure.
  • Research findings should not be interpreted as proof that every permitted use causes harm.

3. Why food companies use them

  • Products may need to survive months of transport and storage.
  • Preservatives can reduce spoilage, returns and wastage.
  • Antioxidants can slow rancidity in fat-containing products.

4. Additional points

  • Rancidity is oxidation of fats.
  • Newtry's proposed approach is to control moisture and water activity, use suitable stable oils, bake carefully, use protective packaging and set a shelf life based on testing.
