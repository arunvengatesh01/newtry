REFINING

What is refining?

1–2. What and why

Refining is an industrial process that separates a natural food into a more purified component: bran and germ are removed from wheat, molasses is removed from sugar, and free fatty acids and colour are removed from oil. Companies do this for uniform colour, neutral taste, longer shelf life and lower bulk costs.

For sugar, processing can include liming, bleaching, crystallising, centrifuging and drying.

3. What jaggery loses when refined

  • Molasses, a source of iron, potassium, calcium and magnesium
  • Polyphenol antioxidants
  • Trace B vitamins and natural caramel flavour

4. State after refining

It becomes white crystalline sucrose, commonly called refined sugar. Refined flour (maida) is the equivalent for wheat.

5. Why companies choose this state

  • Lower cost and consistent batches
  • Longer storage stability
  • Dissolves cleanly and gives pale biscuits

6. Additional points

  • Removed molasses can be sold separately for food and industrial uses.
  • Refined carbohydrate is generally digested more quickly than intact whole-grain foods.
  • Appearance and consistency are important reasons refined ingredients became common.
