ENDOSPERM IN WHEAT

1. What it contains

  • Starch (about 70–75% of the flour, a mix of amylose and amylopectin)
  • Gluten-forming proteins: gliadin and glutenin
  • Some B vitamins and a little fibre

2. Why it is important

  • Main source of energy for the day
  • Provides structure, crispness and the satisfying bite of a biscuit
  • Supplies most of the grain's protein

3. Without it: possible effects

  • Low energy, tiredness and poor concentration
  • A biscuit without it would crumble and lack structure
  • Endosperm alone (refined flour) is not harmful in itself; the concern is eating it stripped of bran and germ.

4. Rare and professional insights

  • Endosperm is about 83% of the kernel.
  • The aleurone layer is botanically endosperm, but it is milled off with bran.
  • Amylose/amylopectin ratio influences how quickly starch raises blood glucose.
  • Gluten gives elasticity; gentle dough handling helps biscuits stay tender.
