JAGGERY

We use jaggery instead of white sugar

1. What is jaggery?

Jaggery (gur) is made by boiling sugarcane juice until it thickens, then moulding it, with no spinning and no removal of molasses. That is why it is brown and keeps trace minerals that white sugar loses.

2. Why it matters for health

  • Contains small amounts of iron, potassium and magnesium that refined sugar does not.
  • Molasses brings polyphenol antioxidants.
  • Gives a rounder, caramel flavour, so less total sweetener may be needed.

3. Energy sources inside

Sucrose 65–85%, glucose, fructose, molasses minerals; approximately 375 kcal per 100 g.

4–5. Jaggery vs white sugar

Point Jaggery White sugar
Sucrose 65–85%; the rest includes glucose, fructose, minerals and moisture Approximately 99.9% pure sucrose
Clarifying Traditionally with plant mucilage (e.g. okra stem extract) May involve sulphur dioxide, lime, centrifuging and bleaching
Molasses Kept inside the block Spun out and sold as a by-product
Colour Natural brown White after refining
Moisture Absorbs water, so it needs good packaging More stable in storage
Micronutrients Traces of iron, potassium, magnesium and calcium Effectively none

6. One biscuit with jaggery

Assumption: a 15 g biscuit with about 20% sweetener contains 3 g jaggery. This provides approximately 11 kcal and small mineral traces. Three biscuits a day would use about 9 g jaggery, or 3.3 kg per year.

Important: Jaggery is still sugar. The benefit is a better-quality swap, not a licence to eat more.

7. Same biscuit with white sugar

  • 3 g white sugar provides approximately 12 kcal with no meaningful minerals.
  • High total sugar intake over time can contribute to dental caries, weight gain and metabolic health problems.

Rare information

  • Darker jaggery is not automatically richer in minerals; cane variety and soil matter.
  • Jaggery's storage stability depends on its moisture and water activity.
  • Indian white sugar is often plantation white sugar.
